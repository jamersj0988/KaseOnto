Text description provided by the architects. The Hodoš Centre for the Elderly is located in the heart of the village of Hodoš. Together with the school and the municipal buildings it is also becoming a generator of local events for, apart from the residential rooms, it houses common areas that are intended for external users.

The clear volume of the L-shaped first floor part is painted in brick-red colour, providing a modern reference to the surrounding red-brick roofs.
The building seeks a connection with the local context where commercial premises are located on a long allotment with a longitudinal side, while residential premises are parallel to the street. Together, the volumes create an inner atrium or courtyard that is repeated in the new construction: due to the L-shape an inner courtyard is being formed on the lower level, linked through the existing terrain and a cultivated meadow with the surrounding landscape.

The flat roof becomes a rising shed-roof when it reaches the rooms. The rooms thus receive additional sun light and the roof is no longer a monotonous horizontal but a dynamic surface resembling the undulated landscape of Prekmurje. The building’s openness is further emphasised with views of the church tower above the road and of the landscape, as the corridor on the western and eastern façades has an opening in the form of frames to guide the views. The corridor is thus no longer just a communication line but becomes a common socialising area that is connected with nature.

**II. Spatial Relationship Descriptions:**
*   A long, linear block houses multiple identical units, all accessed from a single central corridor. Each unit in this block features a private balcony facing outwards.
*   This linear block is connected at one end to a more organically shaped, multi-functional block that accommodates the main entrance, common areas, and additional circulation.
*   Stairwells are strategically placed, one near the junction of the linear and common blocks, and at least two within the common block, facilitating vertical movement and egress.
*   The common block integrates shared amenities and services, clustering them around circulation paths and opening up to outdoor parking and a potential plaza area, establishing it as the social and functional hub.
*   Private living spaces (units) are clearly separated but directly accessible from the communal and circulation zones.

**III. Overall Spatial Impression:**
The design articulates a clear programmatic distinction between private dwelling units and shared communal facilities. It combines a highly efficient and repetitive linear block for accommodation with a more expansive and fluid block for social interaction and services.

**IV. Design Intent Reading:**
The design appears to prioritize functional clarity and efficiency, utilizing a repetitive module for living spaces while creating a distinct, more open volume for communal activities. This suggests an intent to provide a structured yet social environment, likely for a dormitory, hotel, or co-living facility, where individual privacy is balanced with opportunities for collective engagement.
