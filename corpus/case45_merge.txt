Hinged shutters fold up and down over the windows of this monochromatic care home near Paris by French studio Naud & Poux (+ slideshow).
Naud & Poux designed the metal-clad sheltered housing blocks in Villejuif, a suburb to the south of Paris.
The 9,200-square-metre Antoine de St Exupéry Home for Dependent Elderly People has 160 elderly residents, who all require nursing care.
The private and communal areas of the complex are divided up between two long blocks, each comprising a chain of nine connected volumes.

Residents' bedrooms are housed in four-storey-high gabled buildings, joined by low-rise glazed blocks that contain cafeterias, treatment rooms and lounging areas.
The two blocks follow similar layouts and are connected by a glazed walkway. The regimented organisation of the plan was designed to be clear to the 24 Alzheimers patients among the residents, as well as to mirror the neighbouring properties.

"The volumes are intentionally simple in order to reflect those of the surrounding suburban fabric," said the architects.
En-suite bedrooms are clustered around a centrally located staircase and elevator core in each block. The open stairwell, surrounded by metal railings, affords residents views into the communal lower storeys and gardens.
"The service spaces are distributed along the main circulation areas to ensure that the establishment runs efficiently and that the nursing staff enjoy an effective working tool," said the architects.
Each window in the gabled living quarters features a hinged shutter that slides upwards to form a horizontal shade, and down to completely conceal the windows and offer privacy.

The shutters are coated in the same grooved cladding as the facades to give a uniform appearance.
Inside, the windows and doors have black aluminium frames. Neon tube lighting lines the upper lintels.
Communal areas feature wide expanses of glazing, which open out to decked terraces with integrated seating. A black metal framework that angles over the patios can be used to enclose the space.
The architects also designed a new road infrastructure to service the blocks, ensuring ease of access for nursing staff and visitors.

"A major road shaped all thinking for the building's design, offering residents an augmented human presence thanks to greater nursing-staff availability," they said.

II. Spatial relationship descriptions
- The plan is organized as two primary bands of clustered building blocks (north and south) separated by a continuous central axis that reads as a pedestrian promenade with a long rectangular water/reflecting feature.
- Building blocks are staggered and offset from one another, creating intermediate courtyards and terraces that serve as semi‑private outdoor rooms between wings.
- Vertical circulation cores and entry points are located at nodal intersections of corridors and the main axis, concentrating access where blocks meet the promenade.
- Units are arranged along short internal corridors within each wing, facing either out toward the landscape/streets or inward toward courtyard spaces, producing alternation of public, semi‑public, and private frontages.
- A pedestrian bridge/connector crosses the central axis to physically link the northern and southern clusters and reinforce a continuous circulation spine.
- Landscaped strips, tree lines and stepped terraces layer between building volumes and the street edge, buffering and screening the blocks while creating graduated outdoor zones from public to private.
- Service/support rooms are clustered at the ends or cores of blocks, keeping functional uses concentrated and circulation routes clear.
- The composition emphasizes horizontal continuity (axis and terraces) combined with fragmented vertical clusters (wings) that promote light access and view shafts through the site.

III. Overall spatial impression (1–2 sentences)
The scheme reads as a deliberately fragmented ensemble of low‑rise residential wings organized around a strong linear central promenade and water feature, producing a sequence of interconnected courtyards and terraces. The layout balances shared communal space along the spine with sheltered, semi‑private outdoor rooms created by the staggered block arrangement.

IV. Design intent reading
- Encourage social interaction and pedestrian movement by centering the design on a continuous landscaped spine and bridge that link all building clusters.
- Generate varied outdoor environments (reflecting pool, public promenade, semi‑private courtyards, private terraces) to create layered experiences of publicness and privacy for residents.
- Maximize daylighting, natural ventilation and visual permeability by breaking the mass into offset wings rather than a single monolithic block, while preserving green buffer zones along the perimeter.
- Concentrate vertical circulation and service functions at clear nodes to simplify wayfinding and keep living areas efficient and uninterrupted.
- Use landscaping and terracing as a spatial device to mediate scale between street, communal areas, and individual units, reinforcing a human‑scaled residential fabric.
