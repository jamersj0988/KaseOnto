Text description provided by the architects. This project is situated on the Littoral Parc site, which is often criticized for having brought too many commercial activities to its strategic location on the riviera vaudoise on the banks of Lake Geneva. The goal of the master plan is to offer a scheme and landscape that create an alternative to the built-up environment of the adjacent commercial zone. The project creates a more unified and welcoming world in which architecture and nature live together.
The new retirement home is an H-shaped building, at the centre of which is an atrium, open on each of the building’s three levels, which houses the service areas and residents’ day rooms. The ground floor, on which the day rooms are located, is designed for the greatest possible transparency to provide a direct extension to the exterior, for both practical reasons and to maximize the view from the building.
The wings of the building house the bedrooms, which are arranged around a central core of bathrooms to free up the full width of the living space and to orientate it towards a bow window. The window forms a seat, from where residents can enjoy the views.
The volume of the building is slightly bowed by a few degrees, as if to welcome the surrounding landscape into the north and south external courtyards. The perforated corrugated sheet metal facades give the entire volume a feel of lightness and vibration that blends with the surroundings of the park.

**II. Spatial Relationship Descriptions:**
The building's spatial organization is strongly centralized around two significant internal voids: a large, 'A'-shaped atrium/courtyard dominating the lower half, and a smaller rectangular void in the upper central portion. These voids are encircled by a primary circulation corridor, which serves as the main artery connecting all other functional spaces. Individual rooms and offices are arrayed along the building's left perimeter, directly accessible from this main corridor. On the right and upper sides, the layout features a 
mix of smaller, clustered support spaces (like restrooms and utility rooms) and larger, more flexible open-plan areas, suggesting diverse programmatic uses. Vertical circulation elements (stairwells) are strategically located at the building's ends and within the central core, ensuring efficient access to different levels and serving as anchor points for the circulation paths.

**III. Overall Spatial Impression:**
The building exhibits a highly organized, inward-looking spatial logic dominated by large, geometrically distinct central voids. These voids serve as focal points, creating a sense of openness and potentially providing abundant natural light and visual connectivity 
across levels, while the surrounding circulation facilitates clear access to a mix of cellular and open-plan spaces.

**IV. Design Intent Reading:**
The design intent appears to be centered on creating a light-filled and visually connected interior environment within a large floor plate. The prominent central atria are likely designed to maximize natural daylight penetration and potentially encourage vertical interaction and orientation, while also breaking down the scale of the interior. The juxtaposition of a repetitive cellular office layout on one side with more varied and open spaces on the other suggests a programmatic diversity, aiming to accommodate both focused work and flexible, collaborative, or public functions.
