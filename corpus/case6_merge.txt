Text description provided by the architects. This care and retirement home has been built in the heart of the Normandy bocage near the village of Orbec. The building follows the sloping curve of the hillside, and is visible from the valley.To reduce the visual impact of this imposing building, we felt it preferable to divide it up. We achieved the desired effect by using the colour green, with the result that the building both blends into the larger landscape and reflects the rural nature of the site. The under-faces of the overhangs and the white walls of the base produce a feeling of lightness.Each of the living units fits into one section of the building, and all are connected to a south-facing street, backed by the hill. This arrangement gives views through the building from one side to the other, with light punctuating the traffic routes and achieving maximum variety. The colour red destructures the space and adds dynamic. We have avoided using the conventional colours of the hospital environment. The building has been designed to enhance the living and walking areas. Its strength lies in its relationship with the landscape.

**II. Spatial Relationship Descriptions:**
The building's plan is characterized by a series of interconnected, fragmented blocks that house the nursing home units and specialized facilities. A central "lower interior street" (1.07) acts as a primary circulation spine, linking various clusters of units and communal services. Multiple courtyards (1.11) and an enclosed garden (1.08) are strategically embedded within the building mass, providing light, ventilation, and direct outdoor access to adjacent units and common areas. Specialized services like physiotherapy (1.04), hairdressing (1.05), and a snoezelen relaxing space (1.06) are clustered near the main entrance (1.01) and along the main circulation path, ensuring accessibility for all residents. The Alzheimer unit (1.03) is distinctively positioned with its own secure enclosed garden (1.08), suggesting a dedicated and protected environment. Terraces (1.10) and residents' promenades (1.09) are integrated throughout, offering various outdoor leisure opportunities.

**III. Overall Spatial Impression:**
The design presents a non-linear, village-like spatial organization that breaks down a large institutional program into smaller, more intimate clusters. This creates a complex yet navigable environment with a rich interplay between indoor circulation paths and integrated outdoor green spaces.

**IV. Design Intent Reading:**
The design aims to de-institutionalize the nursing home experience by creating a fragmented, organic layout that resembles a small village rather than a traditional hospital. This approach fosters a sense of community and domesticity, promoting social interaction through shared "interior streets" and communal facilities. The abundant integration of courtyards, gardens, and terraces emphasizes a strong connection to nature and outdoor spaces, crucial for the residents' well-being and orientation. The dedicated and secure Alzheimer unit with its enclosed garden highlights an intent to provide specialized, safe, and stimulating environments for residents with varying needs.
