Text description provided by the architects. The project is an institution for dependent elderly (nursing homes) of 125 beds located in the town of Carvin in northern France, 90 beds accommodation "traditional" 30-bed unit in living and Alzheimer 5 home beds day.The project takes into account the following points:

- An architecture that reflects the new urban plan prepared by the City of Carvin.

- Establish contact between the internal life of the intitution and the City.

- Create a building that is a life project, offering activities and multiple spaces for each hour, day, season.

- Highlight the many strengths of the site link with adjoining project views, street and square as possible (communication area), liaison with neighboring school. The following criteria have guided us in the development of this project:

- Optimisation of operation

- Streamlining the cost

- Closeness of the land parcelThe program has led us to propose a building in three levels. This arrangement allows us to identify areas of gardens and household vis-à-vis with the Party. Such a building allows a rationalization of the cost by optimizing the foundations, coverage. This compact volume boundary of the project staff travel and offers a local care provision and techniques adapted to a modern operation, with good support residents growing older and dependent by a smaller staff complement.

We have endeavored to provide the elderly a contemporary, clear and dynamic.

**I. Vocabulary Extraction:**
*   **Residential/Patient Units:** Numerous rooms, typically featuring a bed and often an en-suite bathroom, arranged in wings.
*   **Corridors/Hallways:** Primary circulation paths connecting various spaces and units.
*   **Central Courtyard/Atrium:** A large, open, unroofed space at the core of the building.
*   **Communal Dining Areas:** Large rooms furnished with multiple tables and chairs, designed for group meals.
*   **Lounge Areas/Common Rooms:** Spaces equipped with sofas and armchairs, intended for relaxation and social interaction.
*   **Reception/Welcome Desk:** A curved counter located near a main entrance, likely for visitor management.
*   **Administrative Offices:** Rooms containing desks and chairs, distinct from the residential units, likely for staff.
*   **Meeting Rooms/Conference Rooms:** Larger rooms with a central table, suitable for group discussions.
*   **Stairwells:** Multiple points of vertical circulation.
*   **Restrooms/Toilets:** Both private (within units) and public facilities.
*   **Service Areas/Kitchens:** Areas with counters and appliances, often adjacent to dining spaces.
*   **Storage Rooms:** Smaller, enclosed spaces for supplies.
*   **Main Entrances/Exits:** Points of access to and from the building.

**II. Spatial Relationship Descriptions:**
*   The building's layout is primarily organized around a large, central **courtyard**, which acts as a significant orienting element and source of natural light.
*   **Residential/Patient units** are clustered in elongated wings that form the outer perimeter of the building, providing a degree of privacy and direct access to natural light.
*   **Corridors** run along the inner edge of these wings, providing access to the units and creating a continuous circulation loop around the central courtyard.
*   **Communal dining and lounge areas** are strategically positioned closer to the central courtyard and main circulation paths, making them easily accessible to all occupants and benefiting from views into the courtyard.*   **Administrative offices** and **service areas** are integrated into the overall plan, often located near entrances or central hubs to support the facility's operations.
*   Multiple **stairwells** are distributed throughout the plan, ensuring efficient vertical circulation and emergency egress from different zones.
*   There is a clear **layering** of spaces, with the most private units on the exterior, communal spaces forming an intermediate layer, and the central courtyard serving as a shared outdoor core.

**III. Overall Spatial Impression:**
The building exhibits a highly organized and functional layout, centered around a large courtyard that serves as both a focal point and a source of light and orientation. It appears to be a facility designed to balance individual privacy with ample opportunities for communal interaction and efficient circulation.

**IV. Design Intent Reading:**
The design communicates an intent to create a supportive and well-lit environment, likely for a care facility, hotel, or large office complex. The central courtyard aims to enhance occupant well-being by providing natural 
light, views, and a sense of openness, while also serving as a clear navigational anchor. The strategic placement of communal areas encourages social interaction and community building, contrasting with the privacy offered by the individual units. The overall layout emphasizes clear circulation, functional zoning, and accessibility, suggesting a design focused on operational efficiency and user comfort.
