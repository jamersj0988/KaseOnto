Text description provided by the architects. This retirement home is being built in Monconseil’s Urban Development Zone (ZAC in France), in the northern suburbs of the city of Tours, where this building will be one of the first footprints in the area. The project, which consists of 81 beds spread across three units, was defined from the beginning by specifications that sought to take up and organize an ideal amount of the site’s surfaces. Respecting these specifications to the letter would have led us to a ground floor organization around internal courtyards spread over the entirety of the site.

To try and reconcile these demands with the ZAC regulations the area was subject to, we proposed a compromise. The result of which is a building in the shape of a “U”, composed of three parts. Its main body, made up of three floors, extends out to the alignments required at the southern and eastern boundaries. Its upstairs floors contain the two units for traditional accommodation, while its ground floor contains communal living areas. It is partially built upon stilts, at the corner of two streets, so as to free up, on the ground floor, an entrance area for the public. The second body of the building, made up of a ground floor only, extends to the western boundary, where alignment was also required. It houses all the technical premises, along with the central kitchen, and is extended by a service courtyard. A third and last body, less restricted by requirements of alignment, unfolds in a single, ground floor to house the Alzheimer units, and contains secure gardens, being set back in relation to the street.
On the ground floor overlooking the street secondary premises can be found. The rooms on all upstairs floors are protected by a set back (Alzheimer unit). All the communal living spaces are placed facing the large internal garden. The corridors leading to the rooms are deliberately well ventilated, becoming naturally lit lanes every four or five rooms, and in the end compose a veritable avenue, where events take place within the generous amounts of natural light, and where there are comfortable views of the garden. Each view toward the outside creating a small lounge conducive for meetings.
The facade is composed of prefabricated concrete, over which we applied a plant pattern, in order to produce a vibration in the surface.

II. Spatial Relationship Descriptions
- The plan forms a continuous perimeter block that wraps around a secured inner courtyard; residential modules are arranged facing either the inner courtyard or the outer streets.
- A large, more public landscaped garden sits outside the northern arm of the block, separated from the building by a pedestrian/vehicular edge and by tree planting; parking is located adjacent to this garden at the north boundary.
- Communal functions (dining/multi-purpose/meeting rooms) are clustered along the western arm and near primary circulation routes, allowing easy access from multiple units.
- Vertical circulation cores (stairs/elevators) are spaced along the corridors, providing direct access from galleries to the units and to the exterior; service and garage access is concentrated at the south-west corner, isolating vehicle movement from primary pedestrian routes.
- Internal corridors create a rectilinear grid: a primary longitudinal corridor connects the length of the site, with perpendicular secondary corridors giving access to repetitive unit clusters — this produces a modular, cellular layout.
- The inner courtyard acts as a semi-private communal amenity, visually and physically connected to surrounding rooms and corridors via entry points and framed openings, while the larger external garden functions as a more public landscape amenity.       
- Trees and planted buffers along the perimeter and within courtyards form layered thresholds between street, public garden, semi-private courtyard and private units.
- Service spaces and back-of-house uses are located away from primary communal areas and inward-facing units, creating a separation between operational and social/residential zones.

III. Overall Spatial Impression (1–2 sentences)
The design organizes program into a perimeter block that creates a hierarchy of outdoor spaces — a public garden outside, and a semi-private inner courtyard enclosed by repetitive residential and communal modules. Circulation is linear and modular, prioritizing access to shared green spaces while keeping vehicular and service functions at the site edge.

IV. Design Intent Reading
- The scheme emphasizes community and social interaction by centering shared outdoor spaces (inner courtyard and large external garden) and clustering communal facilities for easy access, suggesting an intent to promote resident gathering and visual connection to landscape.
- The perimeter-block logic enhances privacy and security for residents while maximizing daylight and natural ventilation into the courtyard-facing units; repetitive modular units indicate an efficient, economical housing strategy.
- Vehicular circulation and parking are deliberately pushed to the periphery to reduce conflict with pedestrian flows and to preserve interior courtyards as quieter social spaces, reflecting a pedestrian-focused, landscape-led approach.
- Overall, the drawings communicate a layered public-to-private sequence and a balanced program of private living, communal amenities, and generous landscaped spaces.
