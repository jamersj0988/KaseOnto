Text description provided by the architects. The project consists in 19 social (low-cost) housing for senior and a common room, located in the middle of a city-garden, typical of the iron making tradition of East French departments.
The plot is a strategic point in the district because of its location at the crossing of central streets.
The building strategy was focused on creating a protective cocoon for the elderly inhabitants but also, on recreating an urban solid structure for the neighborhood and, at a larger scale, for the district.
The project consists in four articulated blocks forming an “L”. A public square was proposed by the architects as a main entrance for the project, to create meeting places and urban valuables.  The common room is the building starting point with its   singular shape and higher volume overlooking the square.
Behind wooden fences, housing design is a contemporary reinterpretation of the semi-detached houses from the nearby surroundings: in form, materials and roofs presenting the two typical slopes of the regional architecture.
The program is organized along private alleys. The atmosphere between gardens aims to be warm and welcoming, inviting to gathering and discussion. A ‘petanque’ field and benches have also been fitted out.
Each dwelling includes common areas and bedrooms at the ground floor, suitable for seniors. A private garden and an entry terrace are also planed according to typologies.

**II. Spatial Relationship Descriptions:**
*   The residential units are predominantly arranged in a linear fashion, forming two long, parallel blocks and one shorter, perpendicular block, all oriented to maximize access and views to green spaces.
*   A clear pedestrian network of pathways runs centrally between the residential blocks, serving as primary access routes to individual units and fostering communal interaction within the development.
*   Each residential unit typically features direct, stepped access from a communal pathway to its entrance, and often includes private outdoor spaces (patios or terraces) both in front and back, providing a layered transition from public to private realms.
*   Extensive green spaces are integrated throughout the site, acting as buffers along the main road, communal gardens between housing blocks, and private outdoor areas for each unit, creating a strong connection to nature.
*   Vehicular access and parking are consolidated towards the periphery of the site, specifically along Rue Lucien Clavel, ensuring a pedestrian-friendly and car-free environment within the core residential zones.

**III. Overall Spatial Impression:**
The design presents a highly organized, linear arrangement of housing units integrated within a generous network of green spaces and pedestrian paths, fostering a strong sense of community and a human-scaled living environment.

**IV. Design Intent Reading:**
The design intent appears to be centered on creating a high-density yet liveable residential community that prioritizes pedestrian circulation and shared green amenities. It aims to foster social interaction among residents through communal pathways and gardens, while also providing clear private outdoor spaces for each unit. The strategic placement of vehicular access on the periphery suggests a desire for a tranquil and safe internal environment, emphasizing a human-centric approach to urban living.
