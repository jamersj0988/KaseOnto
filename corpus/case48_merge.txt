Extension of hall and common rooms in a retirement home in Arbon, Switzerland
The retirement home in Arbon consists of several buildings erected independently of one another. By relocating the existing parking spaces to an underground car park, an accessible park-like exterior area was created to interlink the different buildings with a common identity and integrate them into the neighbourhood.

The south- and west-facing “Stoffel” buildings are turned away from the others and are accessed primarily from the back.

With the new assembly room underneath a wide overhanging roof, the “Stoffel” buildings have now been given an opening towards the street to welcome visitors. The concrete roof, designed along the lines of the existing exterior elevations, creates the required sheltered outdoor area leading to a square which acts as a central meeting point. Consequently, this is also where a separation from the main hall creates an everyday common room for the residents. Now accessible from the existing residential building, the hall is where the expressivity of this architecturally designed extension can be fully experienced.
In line with the existing architecture, the hall has been constructed in exposed concrete. The coffered ceiling lends significance and a festive, almost sacred expression to the hall, balancing out the disparity of the four sides, two of which are closed off while the other two feature floor-to-ceiling glazing. The space is illuminated by skylights to contrast the heaviness of the concrete. Different artificial lighting is used to create various moods depending on the use of the hall. The existing foyer has been complemented and modernised while retaining the materials and colours characteristic of its beginnings dating back to 1980.

II. Spatial Relationship Descriptions
- The new/central assembly hall is inserted between existing building wings and opens onto a landscaped courtyard, creating a direct indoor–outdoor relationship.
- Circulation is organized around a central connector: stair/lift cores and a foyer link the assembly to surrounding rooms and the adjacent residential wings.
- Support/backstage rooms (toilets, storage, servery) are clustered immediately adjacent to the auditorium for functional servicing and discreet access.
- Residential units are arranged linearly in a separate block to the right, connected by a corridor spine; these units are separated from the public assembly space by the courtyard and paths, providing a privacy buffer.
- Outdoor curved pathways mediate movement between blocks, creating a soft, meandering approach that contrasts with the rectilinear building geometry.
- Multiple building clusters (residential wing, institutional/communal core, service/utility areas) are layered so public functions sit centrally, private residences flank one side, and service/support spaces sit beside or behind the public core.
- Shared usage is concentrated around the courtyard and foyer — these act as communal nodes linking circulation and program across blocks.

III. Overall Spatial Impression (1–2 sentences)
The plan organizes a new communal/assembly volume as a central hub that stitches together several existing building wings, while a landscaped courtyard and curved pedestrian paths mediate between public and private zones. The layout emphasizes clear functional clustering (public core, back-of-house, private residences) with direct but controlled connectivity.

IV. Design Intent Reading
- Intent to create a clearly legible communal gathering space (auditorium) that activates the site and provides a focal point for residents/users.
- Integration strategy: insert a new public core that connects disparate existing blocks, improving internal circulation and social interaction without fully merging program types.
- Functional pragmatism: locates service and support spaces adjacent to the assembly for efficient operation and discreet servicing.
- Spatial contrast and experience: juxtaposes a rectilinear, programmatic building insertion with organic landscape paths to soften approaches and enhance outdoor amenity.
- Privacy and separation: keeps repetitive private residential units spatially buffered from noisy public functions via the courtyard and path network, suggesting concern for occupant comfort and program separation.
