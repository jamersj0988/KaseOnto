Text description provided by the architects. The house answers to the needs of its owner who has incapacity, and uses a wheelchair, he is elderly person and he wanted practical small, comfortable and pleasant house.
The plot has a trapezoidal shape, facing the street at north side, one public square at east, one small river in the south side, and one neighbour in the west. The house takes benefit of its interesting relation with the surroundings, but has to provide also privacy.
The whole house is in developed in the ground floor level, and is defined by two volumes that are adapted to the irregular shape of the plot, one of them is parallel to neighbour limit, and the other one is perpendicular to the street, being a little turned respect the other one, defining between them a protected space, sunny, saved from the vision from the public spaces, and open to the green plants of the stream. The non-parallel position between this two volumes benefits to get those aims. And the space defined between them will become an extension of the interior spaces, being another exterior room with sun, but also with a small porch with the green background of the plants of the river.
Recessing the rails of the sliding doors into the floor we allow an easy circulation with the wheelchair from the interior to the exterior.
The budget was low, and the house had to be built as much we can by masonry by the builder chosen by the client, meaning we had used the most simple, basic and easy constructive systems, but used with coherence in the way that they provide its maximum performances. The house was built from the interior to the exterior with successive layers, first the structural brick wall that it’s seen as an interior finishing, then the isolation then an empty space that allows hiding the sliding windows, and at the end another thin wall rendered with lime mortar.
The small house takes the maximum benefits of the characteristics of the place, using the low resources available to provide pleasant and practical spaces to the specific needs of the owners.

II. Spatial Relationship Descriptions
- The plan is organized as two separate volumes: a public/service volume (garage + open living/dining/kitchen) and a private, linear bedroom volume. These are linked by a short, intermediary corridor.
- The bedroom wing is rotated relative to the living wing, creating a diagonal relationship that increases privacy and frames views while producing a sheltered courtyard between them.
- The courtyard/patio sits between the two wings and functions as a shared outdoor living space accessible from both volumes, acting as a transitional/interstitial zone.
- The garage is placed adjacent to the service/kitchen zone for direct functional access and for vehicular arrival from the driveway at the street.
- The living/dining spaces are positioned to open onto terraces and the lawn/landscape, orienting primary social spaces toward the stream and garden.
- Trees and planted buffers line the eastern boundary, screening the site from the street/neighboring property while the larger lawn and riparian planting lie to the south, visually and ecologically integrating the house with the watercourse.
- Circulation is layered: private circulation is concentrated in the linear bedroom block; public and communal circulation occurs through the open-plan living wing and the central courtyard connector.

III. Overall Spatial Impression (1–2 sentences)
The design uses a two-wing composition—public/service and private—connected by a short link and organized around a sheltered central courtyard. This creates a compact plan that clearly separates functions while opening living areas to landscape views and providing a protected outdoor heart.

IV. Design Intent Reading
- Separation of functions: The clear split between a linear bedroom block and a public living block signals an intent to segregate private and social activities, improving privacy and acoustic separation.
- Indoor–outdoor integration: The courtyard and terraces, plus generous glazing on the living side, indicate a priority on seamless indoor-outdoor living and views toward the lawn and stream.
- Site-responsive orientation and privacy: Rotating the bedroom wing appears intended to optimize views/solar exposure and to act as a buffer from the street, using geometry to frame landscape and create sheltered outdoor space.
- Functional efficiency: Placing the garage next to the kitchen and grouping service spaces compactly shows pragmatic circulation planning and everyday convenience.
- Landscape preservation and framing: The plan preserves a broad lawn and riparian planting, using trees and buffers to screen the site and emphasize the house’s relationship to the watercourse, suggesting a design that foregrounds landscape and ecological context.
