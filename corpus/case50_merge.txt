Text description provided by the architects. BmesR29 Arquitectes rehabilitated and added an addition onto the Santa Teresa retirement home. The existing building was an isolated three story volume that projected westward with a continuous facade in street Pica d'Estats. The addition almost doubled the size of the building due to functional needs of the retirement home.
With such a large addition the architects tried to avoid adding more volumetric variations that would appear artificial. They aimed to have the building read as a single volume. They partly accomplished this by using a protective outer solar system of vertical aluminum louver that solved the old problem of intrusion in the original building. These louvers also shape the interior light and with the continuous neutral floors that unify the home and give prominence to the different inputs of filtered light from the outside.

II. Spatial relationship descriptions
- The plan arranges enclosed program in a linear band along the southern edge, with rooms opening northward onto a large central courtyard — promoting direct visual and physical connection to the outdoor space.
- Public and social functions (cafeteria, circular multi‑purpose room, assembly/lecture room) cluster on the west and central portions and have multiple access points to the courtyard and corridor.
- Support and service functions (toilets, storage, mechanical) are clustered around the internal corridor and near vertical circulation cores for efficient servicing and circulation.
- Vertical circulation is placed at both ends (east stair) and centrally (stair/elevator core), creating multiple access flows between floors and distributing movement along the plan.
- The courtyard acts as a shared outdoor ‘organizer’ — rooms on the north edge have direct access/visibility while the southern corridor provides sheltered, linear circulation connecting all spaces.
- The planted row along the courtyard’s north edge creates a soft buffer/edge and frames the outdoor space visually.
- The adjoining building to the east influences the plan by confining the open space predominantly to the north and concentrating enclosed spaces to the south.

III. Overall spatial impression (1–2 sentences)
The design is organized as a linear strip of interior program bordering a central, inward‑facing courtyard, with multiple public functions oriented toward and opening onto this outdoor heart. Circulation is kept linear and sheltered along the south, while the courtyard serves as the communal focus that brings daylight, landscape, and direct access to adjacent rooms.

IV. Design intent reading — inferred conceptual gestures
- Emphasis on indoor–outdoor connectivity: arranging rooms to open onto a generous central courtyard suggests an intent to encourage social gathering, spill‑out activities, and passive daylighting/ventilation.
- Hierarchy of public vs. service spaces: clustering social/communal uses (cafeteria, multipurpose/assembly spaces) along the courtyard edge and grouping back‑of‑house functions along the corridor demonstrates clear functional separation for efficient operation.
- Flexibility and adaptability: presence of a large flexible hall and multiple access points implies adaptability for events, different occupant loads, and varied programmatic uses.
- Distributed circulation and redundancy: stair/elevator cores at multiple locations indicate an intention for robust vertical movement, evacuation routes, and convenient access across the plan.
- Framing and landscape as spatial device: the planted edge and paved plaza act as a deliberate outdoor ‘room’ that organizes the building’s social life and provides a framed landscape amenity.
