Text description provided by the architects. A residential care home for 105 elderly residents has been constructed on Statteggerstraße in Graz on a park-like plot in the immediate vicinity of the Andritzbach brook.
The passive house has no basement due to the challenging ground conditions and because of its location within the Andritz flood zone. It is a composite construction and features controlled domestic ventilation. Load-bearing ceilings and walls are made of concrete while all other structural elements are wood. The façade elements, each extending across two rooms, were prefabricated, their size determined by the maximum dimensions suitable for transport.
The two-storey building consists of four wings arranged around a semi-public “village square”, designed to host various events. This is also the location of the roofed vestibule, the central nurses’ station, an oratory, a cafe, a hairdresser as well as an open, tended atrium.
Three residential areas on the ground floor and four on the top floor host one carer and 15 residents each, primarily in single rooms, thereby creating a manageable and friendly atmosphere. A large common living area for each residential group, wide loggias and zoned gardens, some of which are suitable for dementia patients, on the ground floor and atria with tower galleries on the top floor serve as additional living zones. Special attention has been paid to ensure sufficient natural light floods the entire building.
Nurses’ stations and secondary rooms are always nearby, making work processes highly efficient. Shorter and longer walks around the building with varying visual references and pleasant quiet zones ensure diverse free movement opportunities, even outside set therapy sessions.
The interweavement of the building with the outside space has been assigned particular significance. The green and open space was designed with the typology of the care home in mind and is based on a sequence of gardens which differ in size and character. The gardens also form the passage into the park bordered by the Andritzbach to the east. A particularly attractive space for recreation has been created by designing a wooden platform that juts out over the water which, together with the manifold zones of a manageable scale inside and around the building, generates a familiar yet invigorating atmosphere.

- Corridors / circulation corridors (long linear hallways connecting wings)
- Central plaza / main atrium (large open internal space with seating/assembly layout)
- Courtyards / light wells (multiple rectangular inward-facing voids)
- Lounges / common living areas (furniture layouts indicating informal seating)
- Dining / multipurpose seating areas (clustered tables and chairs)
- Service core / back-of-house rooms (larger enclosed rooms at upper right)
- Stairwells / vertical circulation cores (small enclosed shafts at wing ends)
- Elevators (vertical shafts adjacent to cores)
- Terraces / covered decks (hatched areas adjacent to courtyards)
- Meeting / assembly room (arranged seating in rows within central area)
- Small kitchens / pantry zones (adjacent to common areas)
- Restrooms / sanitary rooms (clustered small compartments)
- Storage / mechanical rooms (enclosed ancillary spaces)

II. Spatial Relationship Descriptions
- The plan is organized as an H-/four‑wing composition around a prominent central plaza/atrium: long wings of repeated units sit on the perimeter and face inward toward the shared heart.
- Multiple courtyards/light wells are embedded within each wing and between wings; these courtyards are adjacent to lounges and dining zones, providing daylight and visual connection for the interior common spaces.
- Corridors run longitudinally through each wing and converge at the central plaza, creating a hierarchy of primary (to the plaza) and secondary (to unit clusters) circulation.
- Service and mechanical functions are clustered together in the upper-right sector, separated from the living units but linked by the main corridor network.
- Vertical circulation (stair/elevator cores) is positioned at wing intersections/ends, dividing the blocks into clusters or pods of units and providing repeated access points to the communal center.
- Terraces and covered decks sit at the interface between courtyards and lounges, creating semi-private transitional zones that mediate between public plaza and private rooms.

III. Overall Spatial Impression
- The building logic centers on an inward-focused, communal heart with perimeter living blocks: repeated modular units flank a hierarchized circulation system that funnels activity into a central shared plaza and several smaller courtyards. The arrangement layers private sleeping clusters, semi-public lounges adjacent to voids, and a clearly separated service core.

IV. Design Intent Reading
- The drawings communicate a strategy that prioritizes communal interaction, daylighting, and cross-ventilation: inward-facing courtyards and a central atrium create social nodes and bring natural light deep into the plan.
- Modularity and repetition of units suggest an efficient residential program (e.g., dormitory, care facility, or multi-unit housing) while dispersed lounges, dining/assembly spaces, and terraces indicate an intention to balance private accommodation with varied shared amenities.
- The separation of service areas and clustered vertical cores points to a functional, legible circulation scheme that supports clear wayfinding and operational efficiency.
