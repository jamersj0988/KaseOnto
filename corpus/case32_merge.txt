Veronica House Elderly Care Facility / f m b architekten

Healthcare Center
•
Stuttgart, Germany
Architects: f m b architekten
: Norman Binder, Andrew Thomas Mayer
Year: 
2010

Text description provided by the architects. In the new residential community of the Veronica House, the site for the project is compact. The structure allows the urban transition from the relatively high house No. 47, the former Veronica clinic, to the lower elevation of the southern adjoining residential building.
The ground floor is composed of a public café, a divisible meeting room, a quiet room, and all staff rooms. The administration of the national carrier has taken a room in the attic. In between these two floors lie the three residential floors with apartments and a community house on each floor, each with twelve beds.
The internal structure corresponds to the model of elderly care in home communities. To implement this ideal many things were considered with the design of the building. None of the apartments are oriented to the north, and a large kitchen serves as a versatile place to meet and linger. The kitchen is oriented to the south, facing the garden and the shared terrace.
To allow flexibility for future spatial changes, the building was a reinforced with a concrete frame with nearly every wall of the expansion as a non-supporting, lightweight partitions.

**II. Spatial Relationship Descriptions:**
*   A cluster of private residential units is organized around a central, expansive common area.
*   The central common area serves as the primary hub, directly connecting all residential wings, the kitchenette/buffet area, the assisted bathtub room, and the stairwell.
*   The common area seamlessly extends outdoors to a terrace and further to a large courtyard ("Hof") and a dedicated "Demenz Freibereich," creating a strong indoor-outdoor connection.
*   Individual units are directly accessible from a circulation path that encircles or branches off the central common space.
*   Some units feature private balconies, providing residents with direct personal outdoor access.
*   The kitchenette/buffet area and the assisted bathtub room are strategically placed centrally within the common area, indicating ease of access for all residents and efficient care provision.
*   The courtyard area ("Hof") incorporates spaces for work, games, and reading, suggesting an extension of communal indoor activities into a semi-enclosed outdoor environment.

**III. Overall Spatial Impression:**
The design creates a cohesive and integrated living environment where private residential units radiate from a vibrant central communal core, which itself is highly connected to various outdoor spaces. This layout emphasizes accessibility, community interaction, and a therapeutic connection to nature.

**IV. Design Intent Reading:**
The design communicates an intent to foster a supportive and engaging environment, likely for assisted living or a care facility. The prominent central common area, integrated with essential care facilities (Pflegebad, Arbeitsküche), suggests a focus on communal living and efficient service delivery. The abundant and varied outdoor connections (terraces, Hof, secure Demenz Freibereich, balconies) highlight a strong emphasis on resident well-being, providing opportunities for outdoor activity, natural light, and a connection to nature, particularly catering to the specific needs of individuals with dementia by offering safe and accessible exterior spaces.
