Text description provided by the architects. The structure of the senior citizens residence and the social center is in line with the east-west axis that divides the site into two outdoor facilities with different spatial qualities. The public area that rises between the buildings forms an urban crossing for the senior citizens with the town citizens. The private garden (the “hortus conclusos”) provides the senior citizens with a variety of outdoor activities and, if necessary, constant supervision by the nursing staff.
Relaxing under the fruit trees, taking walks, or caring for the flower beds are just some of the possibilities. The pivotal point of the interior is its multifunctional foyer (reception, dining room, event area, etc.) which is seamlessly annexed to the public space. Earthy and warm tones characterize the atmosphere and are also used as orientation aids within the building. Skylights and large windows admit light and air into the building.

II. Spatial Relationship Descriptions
- The plan separates public and private programs along a roughly north–south axis: public/administrative spaces and the main entrance face the street to the north, opening onto a plaza; residential blocks are set to the south of that plaza.
- Residential units are organized in repeated linear clusters (two rows of modular units) running east–west; each cluster is served by internal corridors and discrete stair/elevator cores that connect to the central plaza/foyer.
- A semi-public plaza (Stadtplatz) functions as the spatial hinge between the street-facing public functions and the more private residential terraces and landscaped courtyard to the south.
- Terraces and garden/play islands are arranged directly south of the residential blocks, forming a communal outdoor layer that buffers units from the adjacent roadway and creates shared amenity space.
- Parking and service access are located at the northeast corner, separated from main pedestrian flows and the courtyard to keep vehicular movement distinct from the central pedestrian plaza.
- Landscaping and tree planting run along the site perimeter, providing a green buffer and defining pedestrian circulation around the building footprint.

III. Overall Spatial Impression (1–2 sentences)
The plan organizes the site into layered zones — public frontage and administrative functions to the north, a central civic plaza, and repetitive residential blocks opening onto a landscaped communal courtyard to the south. The layout emphasizes modular residential 
efficiency while creating clear public-to-private transitions and outdoor shared amenities.

IV. Design Intent Reading
- The design intends to mediate between the street and the housing by providing a prominent public entrance and plaza that anchor the project and give it civic presence.
- The repetition of standardized apartment modules and regular circulation cores suggests an efficient, scalable housing strategy focused on predictability and economy.
- By placing terraces and landscaped play/amenity islands directly adjacent to the residential blocks, the scheme aims to activate shared outdoor spaces and encourage community interaction while preserving privacy for individual units.
- The spatial layering (public plaza → semi-public foyer → private terraces/courtyard) signals a deliberate hierarchy of accessibility and a desire to separate vehicular/service functions from pedestrian, recreational, and living areas.
