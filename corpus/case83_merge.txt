Text description provided by the architects. Nestling in the hollow of a meander of the river Sorne, the building folds to fit the contours of the environment: along the river wide terraces open onto the plant world induced by hydrology; to the north the small volume is in line with the conti-nuity of the existing built landscape. At the heart of this hybrid form is an open courtyard for the institution's senior citizens.
The horizontal distributions benefit from the light provided by this centrality and allow quality pathways to reach one's private domestic space, while serving as a place of reference and location. The concrete construction is expressed in its form in the common spaces where wood blends with the raw material. The prefabricated façades are continuously developed around the complex volumetry.
They display shifted and arrhythmic ver-tical modenatures that refer to the aquatic and vegetal world surrounding the build-ing. The light colour of the texture of the tinted concrete captures the play of shad-ows and reflections that the site enhances through its high quality landscaping.

- Service/technical rooms (small utility rooms adjacent to cores)
- Entrance lobby / access points (main entry zones facing exterior)
- Communal / multipurpose rooms (larger interior spaces near the courtyard)
- Terraces / small balconies or recessed loggias (facing courtyard and exterior)
- Site boundary / landscape (trees and planted strips around building)
- External parking / drop-off area (small parking zone on the site edge)

II. Spatial Relationship Descriptions
- Units are organized in a continuous ring along the site perimeter, accessed from an internal corridor that forms a loop around the central courtyard.
- The central courtyard is the spatial anchor: it is surrounded by circulation and adjacent communal spaces, providing daylight and visual connection to interior-facing units.
- Vertical circulation (stairs and elevator) is concentrated near the courtyard/core so that cores serve multiple wings efficiently and reduce deep plan distances.
- Larger communal or service spaces are clustered toward one side of the plan (left side), separating more public functions from the repetitive private units located on the elongated wings.
- Exterior-facing units open out onto the street/landscape, while interior-facing units and some terraces address the courtyard, creating an interior-exterior layering of private, semi-private, and public zones.
- Parking and vehicular access are placed outside the building envelope near the site edge, keeping vehicular flow separated from the pedestrian courtyard.
- The building footprint follows the irregular triangular site boundaries, creating angled wings and a polygonal void that maximize perimeter exposure and create pockets of outdoor landscape.

III. Overall Spatial Impression (1–2 sentences)
The plan reads as a perimeter block organized around a central landscaped courtyard, with a continuous internal corridor and centralized vertical cores providing efficient access. The irregular, angled footprint adapts to the triangular plot, producing a compact ring of mostly repetitive apartment units with communal functions concentrated near the core.

IV. Design Intent Reading
- To maximize daylight, ventilation, and social interaction by centering an open courtyard that brings light and greenery into the plan’s interior.
- To respond to a constrained, irregular triangular site by angling wings to follow property lines and thereby maximize street and courtyard frontage for each unit.
- To create a clear spatial hierarchy: public/communal functions and vertical circulation cluster around the courtyard, while private units line the perimeter for privacy and views.
- To achieve efficient, legible circulation with a single continuous corridor and few vertical cores, reducing travel distances and simplifying access to all units.
- To provide layered privacy and quality of outdoor space—public landscape on the site edges, semi-private courtyard for residents, and private terraces/loggias for individual units.
