Text description provided by the architects. AVALIAN, a company dedicated to high-quality medical healthcare, in terms with the commitment to accompany its members in the different stages of their lives, asked us to develop this housing for the elderly.
The new building is located on a more than 10,000 m2 site with an interesting and ancient forestation. Designed with a central corridor, an east-west axis that separates and organizes the residential use from services. Residential units are connected from the North to the central axis which, rotated and spaced apart, generate internal yards for views and lighting. To the south and matching with the living units, the services and therapeutic support volumes are connected (day center, gym, occupational therapy, physiotherapy, swimming pool and hydrotherapy area, beauty and hairdressing).
Patient-centered care. Each living unit is designed as an autonomous system under global management, with both residents and human resources in mind, without overcrowding, with a similar ratio of customizable single/double rooms.
A total of six residential groups were created, each with a central nurses’ station. All six living units combines shared and private spaces, using their own support spaces. They have a dining room, living room and lounge room for 12/16 people, achieving a sense of belonging, familiarity and orientation, encouraging exchanges and social interaction.The living units have color coordinated surfaces that provide  orientation for residents and guests.
Outdoor spaces. A key part of the design was given to outdoor spaces, composing them in different sequences and enhancing the therapeutic value of vegetation, sun and fresh air that stimulate active and independent life of residents in safe places.
To the north, the gardens are crossed and connected by paths to walk, with various meeting points to rest and contemplate; and spaces to exercise. Towards the south, spaces are created to carry out more specific activities that invite you to socialize with decks, furniture and an iconic spiral ramp.
Architecture Design. The project´s main aim is to create a pleasant building for senior citizens, a space more in recognition with a homely atmosphere than a hospital environment. It priorities the feeling of domestic scale and well-being; a comfortable, warm, welcoming and safe environment for residents.

II. Spatial Relationship Descriptions
- Multiple residential wings are organized off a continuous central spine: the spine contains communal functions and links all clusters, while short branch corridors feed the individual units.
- Communal spaces (dining/activity/lounge) are positioned centrally along the spine to be readily accessible from all wings, creating a shared core of services.
- Stair/elevator cores are located at regular intervals near the junctions between the spine and each wing, providing vertical access and service separation.
- Vehicular access, drop-off, and parking are concentrated along the lower edge of the site and at a dedicated entrance loop, separated from the landscaped courtyards to preserve quiet pedestrian garden areas.
- Outdoor paths and garden pockets sit between and around the wings, forming semi-enclosed courtyards that are visually and physically connected to adjacent communal rooms and some units.
- Smaller detached pavilions or features are placed within the landscape pockets, acting as destinations or sheltered outdoor spaces associated with the internal program.
- Service zones (kitchen, stores, staff areas) cluster adjacent to dining/activity spaces for operational efficiency, while medical/first-aid is placed near the main circulation for quick access.

III. Overall Spatial Impression (1–2 sentences)
The plan reads as a linear communal spine with repeating residential wings branching off — a modular composition that balances distributed private units with a central public core. The massing is fragmented into smaller wings to reduce scale and to create multiple landscaped courtyards and visual connections between interior communal areas and the outdoors.

IV. Design Intent Reading
- The design aims to foster community and easy social access by locating shared amenities centrally and making them equidistant from clusters of private units — suggesting a program focused on communal living (e.g., assisted living, care residence, or a communal housing project).
- Fragmentation of the plan into wings and insertion of gardens indicates an intent to humanize scale, maximize daylight and views for most units, and provide sheltered outdoor spaces for residents.
- Clear separation of vehicular circulation/parking from pedestrian garden areas shows an emphasis on safety and creating calm, walkable landscape zones.
- Repetition and modularity of unit layouts imply efficiency and standardization in delivery and operation, while the central spine and clustered service cores prioritize legibility, wayfinding, and operational adjacency.
