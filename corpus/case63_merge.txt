Text description provided by the architects. The medico-social support structure (SAMS) welcomes senior residents, for a short stay, before their return home. The second complex of the SILO Foundation in Echichens on the heights of Morges, the new building offers additional services such as a doctor's office for general practitioners, a hairdresser, and a tea room.
The architectural project seeks to fit into the site and respond to the built environment consisting mainly of two poles: the village and the first building of the SILO Foundation. The building tries to play a pivotal role between these two geometries and to connect existing constructions.
The proposed structure is articulated in two main buildings each arranged around a patio. The scale of the project is thus more suited to the rural context.  The facades are made up of prefabricated reinforced concrete elements.
This building is timeless. It is a hospitality structure in the literal sense of the term that goes far beyond the program itself. If the proposed architecture may seem imbued with a certain archaism, it offers a contemporary revisiting of structural themes from vernacular heritage. There is no nostalgia in this approach, but rather a desire to firmly anchor the project in a broader temporality than that of the cultural context in which it is built.

II. Spatial relationship descriptions
- Units are organized in a linear, repetitive module along the exterior perimeter so most units face outward for day lighting.
- Two inward-facing voids (a square courtyard in the left wing and a rectangular atrium/lightwell in the right wing) create semi-public interior space and bring light/air into the centre of the plan.
- Corridors run around and between the courtyards, linking the two wings and providing direct access to each unit; circulation forms a continuous loop around the courtyards.
- Vertical circulation (stairs and elevators) and service cores are clustered near the junction between wings and at strategic ends, minimizing plumbing/shaft runs and acting as connection nodes between levels.
- Sanitary modules are grouped or mirrored within units (paired or compact bathrooms) to optimize servicing and plumbing efficiency.
- Larger/communal rooms (lobby/meeting or shared amenity spaces) are located adjacent to the central atrium, using the internal void as a visual/ social focus.

III. Overall spatial impression (1–2 sentences)
The plan uses a modular, perimeter-based layout with two internal voids to maximize daylight and ventilation while concentrating services in compact cores. Circulation wraps these courtyards, creating a clear separation between private perimeter units and semi-public interior communal spaces.

IV. Design intent reading
- Emphasis on daylighting and passive ventilation: internal courtyards/atrium and shallow unit depths indicate an intent to bring natural light and air deep into the plan.
- Social/communal focus: the central atrium and adjacent shared spaces suggest a design strategy that frames interior communal life around a landscaped or activity-filled void.
- Efficiency and economy: repeated unit modules and clustered service/vertical cores point to an intention for efficient construction, compact servicing runs and cost-effective repetition (suitable for multi-unit housing or a lodging program).
- Clear hierarchy of space: private (units) at the perimeter, semi-public circulation around courtyards, and concentrated vertical/service nodes at junctions, indicating thoughtful circulation sequencing and programmatic zoning.
