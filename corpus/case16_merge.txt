Text description provided by the architects. The elderly care is a part of the city block Skärvet, which is the starting point of Bäckaslöv, a new urban district in Växjö being developed along the railway connecting the city centre to the lake Norra Bergundasjön. The L- shaped building is the first phase and the cornerstone of the city block, offering mixed forms of tenure around a spacious, shared courtyard.Common areas with good light conditions
The layout organisation is based on arranging all common and neutral areas into a central hub with individual departments located in each respective wing. The core of each department is its dining and living room, an open space reaching from façade to façade. Its large windows and tall ceiling heights provide great daylight conditions.paces for social interaction
The common functions comprise an assembly hall, a conservatory, physiotherapy facility and a greenhouse on the rooftop with a view over the shared courtyard and the green recreation space.Lively façades
The design and configuration are strongly linked to the construction process. The variation in the façade is rationally achieved by using more joints than façade elements and by combining four sections of different types of tile patterns. The overall design thus resembles a textile pattern or a crocheted table cloth. Entrances, windows and roofs are accentuated by copper-coloured metal sheets, adding warmer tones. The building is designed to achieve LEED Platinum certificate and Skanska’s top level environmental classification.

**II. Spatial Relationship Descriptions:**
*   **Clustering:** Private units are clustered along the main corridors, forming distinct wings of the building.
*   **Adjacency:** Each private unit is directly adjacent to its own private bathroom. Common living and dining areas are strategically placed adjacent to the main corridors, making them easily accessible from multiple units.
*   **Connection:** The corridors serve as the primary spine, connecting all private units, common areas, and stairwells, facilitating movement throughout the floor.
*   **Shared Usage:** The common living areas, dining areas, and kitchenette are designed for shared use by the occupants of the various units, promoting communal interaction.
*   **Layering:** The layout exhibits a clear layering of spaces: a central circulation path (corridor) provides access to private zones (units) on its periphery, with communal zones interspersed at key points along the path.
*   **Form:** The building adopts an L-shaped or angled configuration, with two main wings extending from a central core that appears to house some common facilities.

**III. Overall Spatial Impression:**
The floor plan depicts a highly organized and efficient layout, likely for a residential facility such as an assisted living home, dormitory, or hotel, that balances private accommodations with accessible communal spaces. 
The design emphasizes clear circulation and a repetitive modularity for the private units, while integrating larger shared zones for social interaction.

**IV. Design Intent Reading:**
The design communicates an intent to create a functional and supportive environment that caters to both individual privacy and community engagement. The modular repetition of units suggests an emphasis on efficiency and ease of construction, while the generous provision of common areas indicates a strong focus on fostering social interaction and a sense of belonging among residents. The clear circulation paths and strategic placement of shared amenities aim to enhance accessibility and user experience within the facility.
