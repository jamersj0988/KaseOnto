Text description provided by the architects. The new building is located at the intersection of two neighborhood streets and forms the corner of a new urban block. The compact volume allows the definition of a central free space, a new resident and public park that links and enhances the various elements: the new nursing home, an existing villa, an annex, a small church.
The hilly landscape of the terrain has allowed dividing the road section where the main entrance is, and the park level, where all the common functions are grouped together. The parking lot was treated as a tree-lined space. The building typology is compact, with a central core. In the latter, there are elevators, stairwells, and the service rooms for staff use.
From the main entrance, through an entrance hall, you access directly to the upper floor, where you can find most of the common functions including physiotherapy, ergotherapy, dining, living room and bar. Here a glass facade puts the park in close relationship with the interior spaces, while a porch provides a protected area and mediates the relationship between interior and exterior. On the top three floors, there are 76 single rooms, each with service and shower, living room, dining room, service rooms and various medical facilities.

II. Spatial Relationship Descriptions
- The program is organized as a linear bar running roughly along the lower site edge, parallel to the street; rooms and smaller functions line the street-facing façade.
- A cluster of public/communal functions (large open-plan hall and adjacent terrace) is positioned toward the north-central portion of the plan, directly opening onto the terraced landscape — creating a strong indoor–outdoor relationship.
- The vertical circulation and service cores are centrally placed within the bar, acting as a hub that connects private rooms/offices, the large hall, and support spaces — facilitating efficient internal circulation.
- The auditorium/lecture room is located at one end of the bar, slightly set apart from quieter office/classroom clusters to limit acoustic interference while remaining directly accessible from the main circulation.
- Outdoor terraces step up the slope via a sequence of retaining walls (contour lines) and are visually and physically connected to interior communal spaces, producing layered outdoor rooms and shared amenity spaces.
- Parking and vehicular access are located on the uphill side, providing direct approach to the building while pedestrian circulation is encouraged along the street-facing side and terraces.
- The orchard / planted grid occupies a separate, contained area at a lower/adjacent terrace, visually linked but functionally distinct from the main building’s social spaces.

III. Overall Spatial Impression (1–2 sentences)
The design arranges functions in a compact linear block that follows the site contours, concentrating service and vertical circulation in a central spine while opening communal spaces toward terraced landscape. The plan emphasizes strong visual and physical connections between interior social areas and layered outdoor terraces, mediating the slope through retaining walls and planted terraces.

IV. Design Intent Reading
- Integrate building with topography: the stepped terraces and retaining walls suggest an intent to work with the slope rather than reshape it, embedding the building edge into the hillside and preserving graded outdoor spaces.
- Promote communal interaction: large open-plan seating/dining and the terrace immediately outside indicate a social focus — the design encourages gatherings, flexible use, and indoor–outdoor flow.
- Clear functional separation and efficiency: centralizing vertical circulation and services creates compact, efficient distribution to offices, classrooms, and the auditorium while isolating noisy or intensive functions.
- Landscape and productive planting as part of program: the orchard/regular tree grid signals an intent to incorporate landscape/food production or formal planting as a programmatic and visual element, reinforcing the building’s relationship to the site.
- Orientation and daylighting: positioning primary habitable rooms along the bar and opening communal spaces toward the terraces implies attention to views and daylight access from the northern/slope-facing side.
