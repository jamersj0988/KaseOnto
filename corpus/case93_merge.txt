Text description provided by the architects. The site was a door in the city wall.

It was a bastion in the third city wall in Barcelona. After this, it became a hospital, a prison, a square and swimming pool.
It is now a residential building providing social housing and dwellings for the elderly people. There is also a passage and a communal courtyard.

The typological scheme is simple: a central corridor with apartments at both sides. In the corners, special spaces are to be found. In the lower floors public facilities are located.
The volume accomplishes two different urban scales:

First, that of the Raval district. The project creates the small square now called Miquel Pallés, which provides a better natural lighting and ventilation to Carretes street. Moreover, the project incorporates the traditional balcony and blind, which gradually filter a relationship of the public and private domains.
And second, the building achieves the urban height of the Eixample buildings at Ronda San Antoni, the avenue that encloses the square in an opposite position to the project.

The site is now an inhabited door between the Eixample and the Raval. The project mediates between both, between past and present, between craftsmanship and technology.

**II. Spatial Relationship Descriptions:**
*   The building is structured as a large perimeter block, defining the edges of the urban site and enclosing a significant central open space.
*   Residential units are predominantly organized along double-loaded corridors, with clusters of units accessed via shared circulation nodes containing stairwells and elevators.
*   A prominent, expansive central courtyard forms the heart of the complex, serving as a large communal outdoor area around which the main building masses are oriented.
*   Smaller, more intimate internal courtyards or lightwells are interspersed throughout the complex, providing essential light and ventilation to interior-facing units and breaking up the massing.
*   Many units benefit from dual-aspect orientations, connecting them to both the central courtyard and either the external street facade or one of the smaller internal courtyards, promoting natural light and cross-ventilation.
*   Private balconies and terraces extend from individual units, creating a permeable layer between private interior spaces and the various communal or semi-private outdoor areas.

**III. Overall Spatial Impression:**
The design presents a dense, large-scale residential development that expertly balances urban massing with a strong emphasis on internal green spaces and natural light. It creates an introspective and communal living environment within a well-defined urban block.   

**IV. Design Intent Reading:**
The design primarily aims to maximize residential density within an urban footprint while rigorously prioritizing natural light, ventilation, and outdoor amenities for residents. The extensive use of courtyards, particularly the large central one, suggests an intent to foster a sense of community and provide significant shared green space, mitigating the effects of high-density living. The repetitive modularity of the units ("Planta Tipus") indicates an efficient and standardized approach to housing provision.
