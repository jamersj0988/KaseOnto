The Elderly Residence, located on the outskirts of Barcelona, aims to solve the transition from an urban area to the Collserola Park. The building shows like an organic volume adapted to the topography, the landscape and the pre-existences. The overhangs reinforce the concept of the ground and the vegetation passing under the building. The pre-existing stone arches and retaining walls determine the main entrance to the new building and also its height.



These listed elements are one of the most important features in the project. The level on top of the preserved wall is the boundary between two different parts of the building, one leaning into the other. The stone lower volume comprises ground floor and mezzanine, and hosts main entrances and amenities. The white overhanging volume contains the two bedroom floors, divided by curved corridors.



A small listed old house in the plot has been also included in the project. It has been completely refurbished, and houses four rooms for non-handicapped customers. These extra quality rooms have even an independent entrance.

II. Spatial Relationship Descriptions:
The design features a prominent linear arrangement of a building complex that follows the natural curve of the site. Multiple hotel units are clustered along a central, slightly curved corridor. These units, along with the main public spaces like the restaurant and 
lobby, are oriented to face outwards towards a significant water body. Extensive outdoor terraces, often stepped, connect the indoor public areas directly to the landscaped gardens and provide a transition zone before reaching the water's edge. A vehicular access road runs parallel to the opposite side of the building, providing a clear point of entry and circulation distinct from the guest-oriented spaces. The swimming pool is strategically placed within the landscaped area, offering views towards the water body and accessible from the main complex.

III. Overall Spatial Impression:
The spatial logic is characterized by a long, curvilinear building form that maximizes outward views and seamlessly integrates with the natural topography. It prioritizes a clear distinction between an arrival/service zone and a leisure/view-oriented zone, linked by a series of terraced outdoor spaces.

IV. Design Intent Reading:
The design intent appears to be the creation of a hospitality complex, likely a resort, that capitalizes on a scenic waterfront location. The linear organization and outward orientation of rooms and public spaces aim to maximize panoramic views and natural light. The extensive use of terraced outdoor areas and a prominent swimming pool suggests a focus on leisure, connection to nature, and an indoor-outdoor living experience, while thoughtfully responding to the site's contours.
