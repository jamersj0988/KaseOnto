The "Residence Alice Guy" is a 5-storey building designed for elderly. It is located in Paris 19th arrondissement of Paris, next to a canal: the "Canal de l'Ourcq".
A residential building widely opened to the city. This building is designed to be perfectly integrated in its surroundings.

Each floor was conceived as a fragment of a city in which:

-The rooms, grouped in clusters of 2,3 or 5 units, would be like the city blocks

-The circulations would represent the streets
-The communal spaces overlook the urban landscape, the canal or the gardens and trees around.

Space Layout

Living in the building is like living in a liner as all the activities are spread over the 5 floors:

-Ground floor : main hall, living-space, animation space, restaurant, administration and doctors

-1st floor to 4th floor :, residents rooms, solariums-green houses, winter gardens and living spaces

-3rd floor, space for care and physiotherapy opened on the winter garden

-5th floor, hairdresser, balneotherapy, Snoezelen space, unit for special care and activities and a terrasse overlooking the city.

The Rooms

The rooms are designed as small housing units. Their facades are composed both inside and outside. Built-in furniture integrates a closet, shelves, storage, a window alcove and a bench for 3 guests.

- Site elements: street frontages (Rue de Colmar, Rue de Thionville, Rue Evellette), sidewalk, trees, planted strips/landscaping, site boundary
- Building form: irregular/triangular footprint, perimeter glazing/façade
- Entry elements: main double-door entrance (south), secondary/staff/service access (northwest)
- Vertical circulation & cores: stairwells, elevators, vertical shafts (three highlighted cores)
- Horizontal circulation: central open lobby/aisle, internal corridors
- Programmatic spaces: large open-plan public space (café/restaurant or flexible workspace with tables and lounge seating), cluster of private rooms/offices/meeting rooms, service/kitchen area, toilets/restrooms
- Structural elements: column grid (dots)
- Outdoor spaces: small terraces/balconies at corners and a planted courtyard/green buffer at west
- Support spaces: storage/service rooms, back-of-house zones

II. Spatial Relationship Descriptions
- The plan organizes program around a large, continuous central open space: seating and lounge areas are concentrated toward the north and central part of the plan and benefit from the long façade windows.
- Three vertical cores (stairs/elevators) are distributed roughly along the central axis, breaking the open plan into zones and providing multiple vertical connections; two cores cluster nearer the center, one nearer the east corner.
- Private rooms/offices and service rooms are arranged along the southern/eastern perimeter, forming a buffered layer between the street (rue de Thionville / rue Evellette) and the open central area.
- The kitchen/service block and restrooms are positioned toward the northeast, adjacent to circulation cores for efficient servicing and concealed back-of-house access.
- Entrances connect the street directly into the central public zone; smaller terraces/green buffers at the west and at the east corner create transitional outdoor thresholds and visual relief.
- Structural columns are regularly spaced across the open area, allowing flexible furniture layouts and clear sightlines across the plan.

III. Overall Spatial Impression (1–2 sentences)
The drawing shows a strategy that prioritizes a large flexible central public space framed by service and private functions along the perimeter, with distributed vertical cores carving the plan into legible zones. The irregular triangular site is exploited to create porous edges and multiple access points while maximizing daylight along the façades.

IV. Design Intent Reading
- The design aims to create a social, flexible interior hub — likely a café, lobby or open-plan workspace — that is highly accessible from the surrounding streets and visually connected to the exterior via extensive glazing and terraces.
- Perimeter placement of offices, service rooms and sanitary facilities suggests an intent to shield the central communal area from back-of-house functions while using cores as organizing anchors for circulation and vertical access.
- The distributed cores and open-plan geometry indicate a desire for modularity and adaptability (easy reconfiguration of furniture and program), while the small planted terraces and green buffer respond to urban context by softening the street edge and providing outdoor spill-out areas.
