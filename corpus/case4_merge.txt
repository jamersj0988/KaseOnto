Text description provided by the architects. Day center and home for the elderly of Blancafort.A social building necessary for the elderly of the village of Blancafort and its neighboring municipalities.The initial observation of the plot and its environment led to consider that the future building did not have to solve only a program and a requirement of an isolated building, but it also had to help consolidate the nearest urban fabric, creating an entrance to the village with its own personality and public character. · Llenar el espacio del solar con un edificio que aporte un patio interior para cada uno de los dos equipamientos públicos. It is proposed to open the building to the three surrounding streets and public green area, turning the preexisting retaining wall into the base of the building and creating a core of common access and an internal courtyard for each of the two public facilities.The Home for the elderly directs its main space to the surrounding landscape, while the Day Center directs its main space to a larger internal courtyard, designed for the gathering together and more leisurely activity of its users.The external finishes respond to not require continuous maintenance. The possible perception of concrete as a cold material is balanced with warm wood finishes, weathering steel and stone that lines the wall socket (used in several places in the same village which is typical from the area) and projected vegetated areas.As for the interior finishes, these aim to provide users with the warmth necessary to enjoy a building designed to be comfortable during the last stage of their life.En cuanto a los acabados interiores, estos pretenden aportar a los usuarios la calidez necesaria para que disfruten de un edificio concebido para hacer más confortable la última etapa de su vida.

**II. Spatial Relationship Descriptions:**
*   The building's primary mass is organized around two distinct internal courtyards, which act as central light wells and focal points for the surrounding rooms.

*   The building's form closely follows the irregular, elongated triangular shape of the site, with its longer side bordered by a sloping landscape indicated by contour lines.
*   Circulation paths appear to wrap around the internal courtyards, offering views into these green spaces and connecting various programmatic elements.

**III. Overall Spatial Impression:**
The design integrates a diverse program within an irregularly shaped site, utilizing internal courtyards to organize space, provide natural light, and create a sense of inward focus. It strongly emphasizes the connection between indoor activities and expansive outdoor terraces, adapting its form to the site's unique geometry and topography.

**IV. Design Intent Reading:**
The design intent appears to be rooted in a deep responsiveness to the site's irregular geometry and topography, shaping the building to fit seamlessly within its context. The inclusion of two internal courtyards suggests a desire to create private, serene microclimates and bring natural light and ventilation deep into the building's core, fostering a connection with nature even indoors. Furthermore, the extensive outdoor terraces, particularly adjacent to the dining area, indicate an intention to blur the boundaries between interior and exterior spaces, promoting outdoor living, social interaction, and an immersive experience of the surrounding landscape.
